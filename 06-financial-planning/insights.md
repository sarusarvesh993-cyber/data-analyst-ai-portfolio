# Stakeholder brief

## Decision

Where should finance leaders focus budget reviews after Q3, and how should
straight-line pacing signals be separated from genuine accounting forecasts?

## Real public-finance findings

- The FY2026 operating-budget snapshot contains 57,267 rows across 40
  departments, 156 funds, and 196 programs.
- Annual budget totals **$8.102B** and expenditures through Q3 total **$6.021B**.
- Citywide utilization is **74.32%**, slightly below the 75% elapsed-time
  benchmark. Pace variance is **-$55.25M**, which is favorable relative to that
  simple benchmark.
- Six departments are more than five percentage points above the Q3 pacing
  benchmark. These are review priorities, not automatically overruns.
- Austin Energy has the largest annual budget (**$1.611B**) and is near pace at
  **74.58%** utilization.
- Personnel and benefits are above straight-line pace at approximately 81.3%,
  but the City's wage-versus-leave coding caveat materially limits object-level
  conclusions.
- 13,688 source rows contain positive expenditures against zero line-level
  budgets, totaling approximately **$435.2M**. These lines remain visible for
  reconciliation rather than being removed or labeled as fraud/waste.

## Recommended action

1. Start review with the six above-pace departments, then drill through funds,
   programs, and management expense categories.
2. Separate timing-driven items—debt service, scheduled transfers, grants, and
   capital-related movements—from recurring operating expenses.
3. Reconcile zero-budget personnel objects to their funded regular-wage and
   personnel-savings objects before escalating.
4. Use the linear run rate only as a screening signal. Replace it with owner
   forecasts that incorporate payment schedules, vacancies, encumbrances, and
   known commitments.
