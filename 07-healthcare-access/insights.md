# Stakeholder brief

## Decision

Which states and union territories should programme managers review first for
healthcare access and readiness gaps, while respecting missing data and mixed
reference periods?

## Findings

- The official report covers 36 states/UTs, 183,562 sub-centres, 26,309 PHCs,
  6,388 CHCs, 784 district hospitals, and 23,187 reported 24x7 facilities.
- Median urban health-facility mapping is 100%, but this national median masks
  states with incomplete or zero reported mapping.
- Median PHC three-nurse readiness is 42.6%, indicating wide reported staffing
  variation.
- Infant mortality is available for all 36 states/UTs, with a median of 14.5
  per 1,000 live births in the report.
- Ten states/UTs fall in the higher review-priority quartile. Uttar Pradesh and
  Odisha lead the screening index because multiple access, staffing, mapping,
  and outcome signals align.
- Population-dependent rates are unavailable for Telangana and Ladakh; these
  are data-completeness issues, not zero need.

## Action

Use the index to organize review meetings, not allocate funding automatically.
Validate high-priority signals with current population, beds, vacancies,
travel-time, service utilization, and district/facility records. Resolve source
inconsistencies before comparing small changes or publishing league tables.
